02 / What the employee does

Return a key

  1. Sign in and search the property address. Confirm the exact property, including unit where applicable.
  2. The machine retrieves that property's assigned empty carrier and presents it at the front port.
  3. Place the key in the carrier and confirm the return. No loose key drops into moving machinery.
  4. Close the public hatch. The system checks the carrier ID, key presence and door state, then files the carrier at its known address.

Retrieve a key

  1. Search the address and select an available key.
  2. The controller reserves the carrier and brings it to the front exchange chamber.
  3. After the inner gate is confirmed closed, the public hatch opens. Remove the key.
  4. Close the hatch. The empty carrier returns to its assigned slot and the checkout is recorded against the employee.

The screen workflow is a specification. The interactive Anatomy displays mechanism poses; it does not perform live inventory transactions.

03 / How the carrier moves

PoseRequired motion and condition
IndexPark the fork and any carried tray outside the rotor's swept circle. Index the requested column and positively verify the rotor is stationary and held.
EngageApproach 5 mm below the seated carrier. Extend 200 mm, then raise 5 mm so the fixed pin enters the underside socket.
UnseatRaise another 3 mm to clear the 2 mm retaining peg from the shelf.
Retract and travelRetract 200 mm before moving vertically. Bring the lifted carrier base to 973 mm at the exchange level.
HandoffWith the public hatch closed and locked, open the inner gate. Extend 220 mm into the chamber.
ReleaseLower the fork 8 mm from the lifted carry pose. The carrier seats at 970 mm; the fork top ends at 965 mm and its fixed pin tip at 968 mm. Withdraw the fork.
PresentConfirm the fork is clear, close and verify the inner gate, then unlock the public hatch. The reverse sequence collects the carrier.

Nominal diametral clearances are 1 mm at the retaining peg and 2 mm at the pickup pin. Those are design dimensions, not an approved manufacturing tolerance stack.

04 / What was checked in ClassCAD

Geometry and identity

The native assembly was saved and reopened. All 1,120 carrier addresses and all 28 shelves were checked for correct placement; no carrier placement faults were found.

Shared carrier and shelf templates keep the full machine manageable without replacing the 1,120 physical positions with a capacity label.

429 sampled checks

350 extraction and lift checks plus 79 final handoff, engagement and shutter checks passed the configured 1 mm³ interference threshold. The largest residual in the final handoff checks was approximately 9.7 × 10⁻⁸ mm³.

Two sensor/cantilever collisions were found during development and corrected by moving the inner gate sensors. These results are sampled geometry checks, not proof of every continuous path, structural capacity or safe operation.

05 / Rentvine and inventory

Use Rentvine's stable property identifier as the property reference. Keep the address as searchable display text. Store the carrier ID, column, level, key state and current custodian separately. Duplicate addresses and units must be resolved before reserving a carrier.

One transaction at a time

Reserve the carrier before motion. Record each confirmed physical step in a durable local journal. Commit a return only after the carrier is verified in storage; commit checkout after removal and hatch closure are confirmed.

Identity and recovery

A carrier reader proves which container is present. It does not prove that an untagged loose key belongs to that address. Require employee confirmation or an individually tagged key. Presence sensing alone is not key identification.

After power loss, reconcile actual gate, fork, carrier and key state before accepting another request. Do not infer success from an interrupted command.

06 / Engineering needed before fabrication

  • Select rated bearings, a bidirectional telescoping mechanism, lift drive, rotary drive, brakes and load-holding devices. Current CAD blocks show component envelopes.
  • Complete transmission geometry, mounting plates, fasteners, cable routing, tolerance stacks, deflection calculations and anti-tip / floor anchorage design.
  • Validate carrier retention, key containment, missed pickups, mis-seating, wear, contamination, jams and recovery access.
  • Engineer and validate independent door interlocks, access protection, emergency stops and power-loss behavior.
  • Build a carrier, shelf segment and exchange-chamber prototype; measure loads and repeatability, then endurance-test before building the full tower.
  • Resolve differing CAD/export mass calculations before using a mass or shipping-weight figure. No verified weight is claimed.
  • Implement and test Rentvine authorization, address search, inventory events, auditing and recovery with a real hardware controller.
